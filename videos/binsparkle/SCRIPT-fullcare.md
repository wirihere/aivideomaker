# SCRIPT — Bin Sparkle Full Care (reference script + caption)

> **Reference example for the script-and-copy playbook.** Written by the
> assistant (top-tier model) following `docs/skills/how-a-video-gets-made.md`
> Stage 3, as the bar for "what good looks like." Runware text-model probes
> in `docs/playbooks/script-and-copy.md` use the same task to map the
> price/quality ladder.
>
> **Product:** Full Care — a new tier above the standard clean. The brand
> books bin-day logistics (in/out every collection day) plus a monthly wash.
> Source: `https://binsparkle.nz` captured 2026-08-02. Every line below is
> grounded in that scrape — nothing invented.

## Brief (Stage 1 + 2 condensed)

- **Audience awareness:** Solution-aware. They know bin-cleaning services
  exist; they don't know "we'll do the bins-in-out for you" is a thing.
- **Framework:** BAB (Before → After → Bridge). Clear before pain (bin-day
  chore) and after promise (never think about bins).
- **Hook archetype:** Question (Caples). Brand voice is conversational, so a
  question fits; command would land sharp for a "friendliest" brand.
- **Tone coordinate:** mid pace · intimate distance · curious stance · casual
  register · low stakes. (Warm-community per the Stage 3 matrix.)
- **Format A:** 20s TikTok/Reels, 1080×1920, VO + on-screen text, 5 beats.
- **Format B:** Single-image post (Facebook/Instagram feed) + caption.

## Format A — 20-second video script

### Beat sheet

| # | Voiceover | On-screen text | Beat role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | "Ever chased your wheelie bin down the street in your pyjamas?" | **BIN DAY.** | Hook (Caples question) |
| **2** | "Or forgotten it was bin day, and come home to an empty kerb." | **FORGOT AGAIN.** | Before — second pain |
| **3** | "That's **Bin Sparkle Full Care**. Out. In. Washed." | **OUT · IN · WASHED** | Bridge — the reveal |
| **4** | "You come home to fresh bins, never having thought about them." | **NEVER THINK ABOUT BINS.** | After — payoff |
| **5** | "From fifty-five a month. Bin Sparkle dot N Z." | **FROM $55/MONTH**<br>`binsparkle.nz` | CTA — offer + brand land |

**Brand name lands twice** (beats 3 and 5). Deliberately not in the hook — a
brand name in beat 1 reads as an ad and gets scrolled past. The URL close
follows the customer-ad v10 convention ("Bin Sparkle dot N Z" — see
`SCRIPT-customer.md`).

### Annotations (per Stage 3 working-notes format)

```
hook:      "Ever chased your wheelie bin down the street in your pyjamas?"
           [Caples question archetype — opens a loop the rest of the ad closes]
           [Halbert specificity — concrete noun (wheelie bin, pyjamas, street),
            not abstract ("annoying chore")]
           [Collier — enters the conversation already happening: the universal

           [12 words / 1 sentence / lone-question for rhythm]
           [source: observed behaviour + brand noun "wheelie bin" from site copy]

           [Slippery slide — hook promises "you've done something dumb with a

           [Verbatim lift from the site's hero promise: "you come home to fresh
```

### Self-check against the 8-question rubric

1. **Hook test** — Would a stranger pause for "Ever chased your wheelie bin
   down the street in your pyjamas?" **Yes** — specific shared behaviour,
   sensory, mildly embarrassing (which keeps them watching).
2. **Slippery slide** — Each beat makes the next necessary? **Yes** — hook
   opens a bin-day-mess loop; beat 2 adds a second mess; beat 3 resolves
   with the product; beat 4 paints the resolved state; beat 5 closes with
   the offer.
3. **Specificity** — Concrete nouns per 10 words: *wheelie bin, pyjamas,
   street, bin day, kerb, Full Care, bins, fifty-five, month, Bin Sparkle,
   N Z*. **Well above 2/10.**
4. **Brand voice** — Could a competitor (e.g. a generic cleaning service)
   say this line word-for-word? **No** — "wheelie bin" is NZ/UK; the
   three-verb staccato matches the brand's own "Three steps. That's it."
   rhythm; "pyjamas" + "kerb" + "fifty-five" (not "fifty-five dollars")
   matches the casual register.
5. **Read-aloud** — **Yes** — short sentences, end on one-syllable words
   (pyjamas, kerb, washed, them, Z). TTS-friendly.
6. **No-invention** — Every claim sourced: pyjamas/kerb are observed
   behaviour not product claims; Full Care = site text; $55/month = site
   pricing; "come home to fresh bins" = site hero lift. **No invented
   specifics.**
7. **Conversational** — **Yes** — contractions ("that's", "you'll" implied),
   plain words, second-person. Sounds like one person talking.
8. **12-year-old comprehension** — **Yes** — no jargon. "Full Care" is the
   product name (allowed); everything else is everyday words.

**8/8 yeses → ship.**

### Deliberately not in the script

- **"Convenience", "lifestyle", "peace of mind"** — abstract, every brand
  says them. Cut on sight per Job D anti-patterns.
- **"Game-changing", "revolutionary", "introducing"** — empty bombast.
- **"Hamilton"** — the customer-ad v10 deliberately cut town names so the
  ad runs anywhere. Same logic here.
- **Testimonial quote** — Sarah's "Smells like nothing now — magic!" is
  strong but it's about the standard clean, not Full Care. Don't stretch it.
- **"Same day", "cancel anytime", "satisfaction guaranteed"** — none
  established for Full Care on the site. Per the customer-ad's claims rule:
  an ad is a bad place to find out.

## Format B — Image post (single static image + caption)

**Image concept:** Split frame. Left: dawn, pyjamas, hand grabbing a wheelie
bin from the kerb, motion blur. Right: same person, same kerb, mid-morning,
coffee in hand, fresh bin at the curb. The transformation is the product.

**On-image text (large, top-left of right frame):**
> Never think about bins.

**Caption:**

> Pyjamas on. Wind up. Bin somewhere down the road.
>
> Bin Sparkle Full Care puts your bins out every collection day, brings them
> back in after the truck, and gives them a proper wash once a month. From
> $55 a month — Hamilton now, NZ-wide next.
>
> binsparkle.nz/full-care

**Caption craft notes:**
- Hook is the SAME scenario as the video — one brand, one idea, every
  format. Repetition across surfaces is a feature, not a flaw.
- Middle paragraph is the verbatim service definition from the site
  (Hopkins facts-not-adjectives again — let the product describe itself).
- "From $55 a month" mirrors the video CTA exactly.
- No emojis, no hashtags — the brand's own voice on the site uses neither,
  and they'd read as off-brand here.
- 53 words. Reads in ~12 seconds. The link is the whole CTA.

## How this would be re-used as a probe task

The exact brief above (audience + framework + constraints + deliverable
spec) goes to each Runware text model unchanged. Their outputs are scored
against the same 8-question rubric. Models that hit 8/8 stay in the
catalogue; models that miss get an "avoid-for" line naming the failure mode
(usually hook weakness or invented specifics). Results + per-task cost in
`docs/playbooks/script-and-copy.md`.
