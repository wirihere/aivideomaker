# SCRIPT.md — Resurgence Heritage Straight Indigo Sport · 30s vertical

The story backbone. Every claim traces to verbatim copy on resurgencegear.co.nz/product/mens-indigo-blue/ — no invented facts (per memory rule).

## Spine

> **They look like denim. They protect like armor. They're CE Triple-A. World-record 10.83-second slide. From $379. Resurgence.**

That's the whole pitch. 16 words. Everything else fills time + visual interest.

## Tone

Confident, clipped, fact-led. Each line ends on a hard period. No questions, no hedges, no "discover" or "introducing"-bait. The world-record claim does the lifting — every other line earns its place by either (a) extending that claim, (b) addressing a buyer objection, or (c) closing.

## TTS hygiene (per memory rules)

- "CE AAA" → "C E Triple A" (TTS pronounces letters cleanly).
- "10.83" → "Ten-point-eight-three" (digits pronounced as words for safety).
- "D3O" → "D-three-O" (otherwise reads as "deeoh").
- "$379" → "three-seventy-nine" (currency symbol pronunciation is unreliable across voices).
- No te reo Māori — already none in the brand copy. Compliant.

## Hook strategy

Per `docs/social-video-patterns.md` R1 + Pattern 1 + the `direct-response.md` AIDA / scroll-stopper rule:

> "The hook word is one of: the pain word, the desire word, or the brand-defining contradiction."

The brand-defining contradiction is the first two lines: **"They look like denim. They protect like armor."** That's the entire elevator pitch — and it's the hook.

The brand wordmark / R*G logo is held back until beat 6 (CTA). No "INTRODUCING" framing. The product photo + the contradiction copy IS the hook.

## CTA

- Verb: implicit ("buy"), not spoken
- Place: kindred-nz.org wait, no — `resurgencegear.co.nz`
- Reason now: "From $379" — price is the trust signal here, not urgency

Per Kennedy's rule from `direct-response.md`: **one verb, one place, one reason.**
