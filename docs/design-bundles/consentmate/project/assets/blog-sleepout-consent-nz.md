# Do I need a resource consent for a sleepout in NZ? (2026 guide)

**Short answer:** Most small sleepouts (≤30 m², single-storey, not used as a separate dwelling) don't need a resource consent OR a building consent if they meet the Building Act's "exempt building work" rules. But the exact answer depends on your council's district plan, your section's zone, your boundary setbacks, and whether it's going to be used for sleeping. This guide walks you through the decision tree used by the 22 biggest NZ councils so you can get a first-cut answer before paying a planner $3,500+ for the same thing.

---

## The two different consents (don't confuse them)

| | **Building consent** | **Resource consent** |
|---|---|---|
| What it checks | Is the building structurally safe and code-compliant? | Is the building allowed on this site under the district plan? |
| Who issues it | Your territorial council's Building Control team | Your council's Planning / RMA team |
| When you need it | Most construction that's not explicitly exempt under Schedule 1 of the Building Act 2004 | When your project breaches a district plan rule (zoning, setbacks, height, coverage, etc.) |
| Typical cost | $800–$3,000+ council fees + $2,000–$6,000 designer/drafter | Usually $1,500–$8,000 consultant + $1,000–$3,500 council fees |
| Timeline | 20 working days statutory | 20 working days (non-notified), months (notified) |

Important: you can need one, both, or neither. This guide is about **resource consent** — the planning-rules one.

## The 5-question decision tree (run these before you spend a dollar)

### Q1: Is your sleepout ≤30 m² in floor area and single-storey?

If yes, and it's not being used for sleeping by anyone other than household members, AND it's more than 1 m from any boundary, AND it's not over a drainage easement — you might fall under Schedule 1 of the Building Act 2004 (no building consent needed). That **doesn't** automatically mean no resource consent, though — the district plan still applies.

### Q2: Is your section zoned Residential (most common) or Rural / Rural-Residential?

- **Residential zones** usually allow an accessory building like a sleepout as a "permitted activity" IF it stays inside permitted limits (setbacks, height, coverage, impermeable surfaces).
- **Rural zones** often treat sleepouts as "minor residential units" and have stricter rules around water supply, wastewater, and minimum lot sizes.
- **Business / Industrial / Open Space** zones — sleepouts usually need consent by default.

Check your council's GIS viewer (e.g. Auckland Council GeoMaps, New Plymouth District Council's MapOnline) for your section's zone.

### Q3: Will the sleepout breach any of these common district-plan rules?

For a typical residential zone, the rules usually say something like:

- **Boundary setback**: minimum 1.0–1.5 m from side and rear boundaries, 3.0–4.5 m from the front (road). If your sleepout will sit closer than this, you'll likely need consent.
- **Height to boundary (recession plane)**: imagine a 45° line rising from 2.5 m above each side boundary — your sleepout can't poke above it. Most 2.4 m-high sleepouts 1.5 m from a boundary pass. Closer than that and you're in consent territory.
- **Building coverage**: the total footprint of all buildings on the section can't exceed a percentage (usually 35–50% in residential zones). Adding a sleepout to a section that already has a house and garage might push you over.
- **Impermeable surfaces**: concrete/asphalt/roofs combined can't exceed another percentage (often 60%). Consent required if over.
- **Height limit**: usually 8 m in residential zones. A single-storey sleepout is well under.

If any ONE of these is breached by your plan, you need a resource consent. If all pass, you likely don't.

### Q4: Is it going to be used as a separate dwelling (e.g. granny flat, rental, Airbnb)?

If yes, the rules tighten significantly — you're now applying for a **minor residential unit** or **second dwelling**, not a sleepout. Most councils need resource consent for this regardless of size, plus wastewater / three-waters assessment. Budget: $3,000–$8,000+ in fees and consultants.

### Q5: Any of these special overlays on your section?

- **Coastal environment / coastal hazard area**
- **Flood / overland flow path / ponding area**
- **Significant natural area (SNA) / ecological overlay**
- **Heritage / character / archaeological overlay**
- **Waahi tapu / sites of significance to mana whenua**
- **Outstanding natural landscape or feature**
- **Designated land (e.g. road widening, Transpower lines)**

If your section has any of these (shown on council GIS maps), consent requirements get stricter fast. Expect a "restricted discretionary" or "discretionary" consent status minimum.

## How to get the actual answer in 10 minutes (free)

1. **Find your property's zone**. Go to your council's GIS map. Type in your address. Click your property. Note the zone (e.g. "Residential - Single House Zone").

2. **Find your council's district plan**. Search "[Council name] district plan" → find the relevant chapter for your zone.

3. **Check the "permitted activity" rules** in that chapter for accessory buildings. They'll list setbacks, height to boundary, coverage, etc.

4. **Measure your proposed sleepout's distance to boundary** and check against the permitted setback.

5. **Check for overlays** on the GIS map — toggle on coastal, flood, and heritage layers.

If you pass all permitted rules → no resource consent needed. If you break one → consent needed.

## Common mistakes that cost homeowners thousands

1. **Starting construction without checking** — council can issue a stop-work and require retrospective consent (more expensive than doing it properly).
2. **Confusing building consent with resource consent** — doing the building one but missing the resource one leaves you legally exposed.
3. **Assuming "it's only small" means exempt** — the Building Act exemptions are specific and unforgiving. 30 m² is the ceiling, not a soft limit.
4. **Not checking overlays** — a 2 m coastal setback breach can turn a permitted activity into a notified resource consent (hearings, six-figure cost).
5. **Ringing three different planners and paying three different quotes** — get a free first-cut from a tool like ConsentMate, then only ring a planner if it's genuinely complex.

## FAQs

**How long does a resource consent take?**
20 working days for non-notified residential consents (statutory timeframe). If council requests more info, that clock pauses. Add 2–6 weeks prep time before submission. So total: ~2–3 months start to finish is common.

**Can I appeal a declined consent?**
Yes, to the Environment Court. It's expensive (lawyer + planner + $5K+ filing fees). 95% of homeowner-grade resource consent applications are approved (most declines happen at the pre-application stage).

**What if my neighbour objects?**
For non-notified consents, neighbour objections don't automatically block approval — council only considers them if the application is "limited notified" to affected parties. Getting written approvals from affected neighbours before lodging drops the risk to near-zero.

**Is ConsentMate a licensed planner?**
